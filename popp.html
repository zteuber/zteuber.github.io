Predictors and Outcomes of Parental Burnout and Parental Educational Involvement

funded by the University of Luxembourg, May 2023 – May 2025

The POPP project investigates the predictors and outcomes of parental burnout and parental educational involvement using a longitudinal application of the Job Demands–Resources framework. The project focuses on parents of lower secondary school students (Grades 6–9), whose children are undergoing puberty. This developmental period is characterized by significant cognitive, social, and emotional changes, as well as shifts in parent–child relationships. As adolescents’ need for autonomy increases, parents face new challenges in supporting their children, which often leads to changes in both the level and nature of parental involvement in education.

The Job Demands–Resources framework offers a flexible structure for integrating a wide range of parenting-related factors. However, it does not fully specify the underlying psychological mechanisms through which these factors operate. To address this limitation, the POPP project complements the demands–resources perspective by integrating relevant middle-range theories to better explain the processes linking parental demands, resources, burnout, and educational involvement.

We conducted the POPP study between September 2023 and September 2024. A total of 1,373 U.S. parents of adolescents aged 12 to 15 participated in the study. POPP employed a four-wave panel design with three-month intervals between waves. In addition, an intensive longitudinal study was conducted with a subsample of participants between Time 3 and Time 4.
